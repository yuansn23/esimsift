Dual SIM is the feature that makes travel eSIMs painless: your phone holds two lines at once — the home SIM you already have, and the travel eSIM carrying cheap local data. Calls and texts keep arriving on your number; maps and streaming run on the eSIM; and no one swaps a physical card at an airport counter. This guide is the two-lines-one-phone setup, including the three settings that prevent the classic bill shock.

## How dual SIM works with one eSIM and one physical SIM

Modern phones run Dual SIM Dual Standby: both lines are registered on the network at the same time, and the phone routes each event by rules you set — which line makes outgoing calls, which receives them, and which carries data. The typical travel split:

- **Home line (physical SIM):** active, in its slot, doing what it always did — receiving calls and SMS on your number.
- **Travel line (eSIM):** installed before departure, carrying all mobile data abroad at local-eSIM prices.

The key mental model: *data* is a switch (one line at a time), *calls and SMS* are per-line (both keep working). Nothing about the home SIM changes except a setting you'll turn off in a moment.

## Which iPhones and Androids support dual SIM with eSIM

Essentially every phone with eSIM support does dual SIM — the feature pair ships together. iPhone XR/XS and later (with regional exceptions for mainland-China models), Google Pixel from 3, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later (US variants included), and most recent flagships from Motorola, Xiaomi and others. The 30-second `*#06#` EID test in our [compatibility check](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) settles your specific device; recent iPhones can even run two eSIMs with no physical card at all.

## How to keep your home number active while using eSIM data

The setup takes one minute and is best done before departure:

1. **Set the data line to the eSIM.** On iPhone: *Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data →* pick the eSIM. On Android: *Settings → Network & internet → SIMs → Mobile data* → the eSIM.
2. **Turn Data Roaming ON for the eSIM line only.** This is the toggle that lets the eSIM use foreign partner networks. Without it, the eSIM shows signal and loads nothing.
3. **Turn Data Roaming OFF for your home line.** This is the toggle that prevents bill shock. With it off, the home line can receive calls and SMS abroad but can never silently burn roaming data.

Calls: leave the home line as default for outgoing calls, or set the eSIM if your plan includes calls. Texts arrive on either line regardless. If your home carrier offers Wi-Fi Calling, enabling it abroad also keeps outbound calls cheap on your home number — check their terms, since a few carriers treat Wi-Fi Calling as roaming.

## Data roaming settings explained

The two roaming toggles do different jobs and get confused constantly:

- **Data Roaming (eSIM line): ON abroad.** The eSIM's partner networks are foreign networks; without this toggle it cannot use them at all.
- **Data Roaming (home line): OFF, always, abroad.** Your home carrier's roaming rates apply the moment the home line uses foreign data. Off means off — calls and incoming SMS still work.

One more trap: "Automatic" network selection on the *data* line can occasionally grab a non-partner network. If the eSIM connects but crawls, switch its network selection to manual and pick from the provider's listed partners on the plan page.

## Dual SIM battery and speed facts

Two registered lines cost a little standby battery — a few percent a day on modern hardware, rarely noticeable next to travel-day screen time. Speed is unaffected: the eSIM's data runs on the same local networks at the same speeds a local SIM would get. If you want zero home-line drain, toggle the home SIM off entirely for the trip's data-heavy days and back on when you need its SMS — nothing is lost or reinstalled, it's a switch.
