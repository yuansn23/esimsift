Dual SIM is the feature that makes travel eSIMs painless: your phone holds two lines at once — the home SIM you already have, and the travel eSIM carrying cheap local data. Calls and texts keep arriving on your number; maps and streaming run on the eSIM; and no one swaps a physical card at an airport counter.

This guide walks the two-lines-one-phone setup end to end: how the radios actually share the work, the three-step configuration that prevents bill shock, and the parts most guides skip — which line carries iMessage and WhatsApp, what Wi-Fi Calling really does abroad, and three real bill-shock scenarios traced to the single toggle that prevents each. New to eSIMs in general? Start with [eSIM basics](/guides/what-is-an-esim/).

## How dual SIM works with one eSIM and one physical SIM

Modern phones run Dual SIM Dual Standby: both lines are registered on the network at the same time, and the phone routes each event by rules you set — which line makes outgoing calls, which receives them, and which carries data. The typical travel split:

- **Home line (physical SIM):** active, in its slot, doing what it always did — receiving calls and SMS on your number.
- **Travel line (eSIM):** installed before departure, carrying all mobile data abroad at local-eSIM prices.

The key mental model: *data* is a switch (one line at a time), *calls and SMS* are per-line (both keep working). Nothing about the home SIM changes except a setting you'll turn off in a moment.

The word *standby* deserves a beat, because it explains a quirk travelers notice. Dual standby means each line is reachable, but on most hardware only one radio transmits at a time — take a call on the home line and the eSIM's data pauses for the duration. A few recent flagships manage simultaneous data on two lines, but that changes nothing here, because you set one line for data either way.

## Which iPhones and Androids support dual SIM with eSIM

Essentially every phone with eSIM support does dual SIM — the feature pair ships together. iPhone XR and XS and later (with regional exceptions for mainland-China models), Google Pixel from the 3, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later (US variants included), and most recent flagships from Motorola, Xiaomi and others. US iPhone 14 models and later ship without a physical slot at all, which makes the eSIM the default second line rather than an option.

Marketing names vary — Apple says Dual SIM, Samsung says dual standby or eSIM plus SIM — but the capability to check for is the same: an EID from the dialer test and a second line slot in settings. The 30-second `*#06#` EID test in our [compatibility check](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) settles your specific device. Recent iPhones can run two eSIM lines at once with no physical card; on most recent Androids one eSIM plus the physical SIM is the working combination, with extra profiles stored but inactive.

## How to keep your home number active while using eSIM data

The setup takes one minute and is best done before departure:

1. **Set the data line to the eSIM.** On iPhone: *Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data*, then pick the eSIM. On Android: *Settings → Network & internet → SIMs → Mobile data*, then the eSIM.
2. **Turn Data Roaming on for the eSIM line only.** This is the toggle that lets the eSIM use foreign partner networks. Without it, the eSIM shows signal and loads nothing.
3. **Turn Data Roaming off for your home line.** This is the bill-shock toggle. With it off, the home line can receive calls and SMS abroad but can never silently burn roaming data.

Doing this at home matters: every toggle lives in a menu you can find calmly on the sofa, and the configuration survives the flight unchanged. At the arrival hall there is nothing left to configure but airplane mode.

Leave the home line as the default for outgoing calls, or set the eSIM if your plan includes calls — most travel eSIMs are data-only, and whether a brand offers voice at all is part of its [provider reviews](/esim-providers/) entry. If the eSIM isn't installed yet, the [install guide](/guides/how-to-install-esim/) covers that half; the rest of this page assumes both lines are on the phone.

## How calls and texts work on two lines

Each line has its own number, its own plan and its own mailbox. What trips people up is only routing — which line the phone picks for what.

### Which line makes your outgoing calls

**Outgoing calls** go out on whichever line is the default. On iPhone you can flip the line per call from the dialer, and both platforms let you set a preferred line per contact — the hotel on the eSIM, family on the home line.

### Which line your texts use

**Texts** send from the line you choose; iPhone asks which line to start new conversations with and remembers per contact. One more routing rule worth knowing: replies stay on the line the conversation started on, so a thread that began on the home line keeps billing as the home line for its whole life.

### How WhatsApp and iMessage pick a line

Messaging apps are the part most guides skip. iMessage, WhatsApp, Telegram and every other one of them ignore the phone line completely and ride whichever line carries data. Abroad that is the eSIM by design. WhatsApp keeps working exactly as at home on your registered number; the eSIM moves its bytes and your home line's roaming status is irrelevant to it. iMessage behaves the same way over data, with one nuance — messages addressed to your home number still arrive because Apple's servers route them, but a friend's plain green-bubble SMS to your home number arrives as an international text, free to receive on most home carriers and worth confirming on yours.

The practical takeaway: everyone who reaches you through an app reaches you over the eSIM's data. Only plain SMS and ordinary phone calls touch the home line — and that is exactly the traffic you want arriving.

## How Wi-Fi Calling behaves abroad

### The trick Wi-Fi Calling gives a dual SIM traveler

Wi-Fi Calling is a home-carrier feature that routes calls and texts over any internet connection instead of the cell network. The dialer behaves normally; the audio moves as data. For a dual-SIM traveler it has one genuinely useful trick: abroad, the home line's calls can ride the eSIM's data connection, so people dialing your home number reach you without roaming voice rates — the call never touches a foreign cell tower.

### Why Wi-Fi Calling billing is unpredictable

Billing policy is the catch, and it varies by carrier rather than by phone. Some carriers treat Wi-Fi Calling from a foreign network as roaming anyway, some restrict it to calls placed to your home country, and a few disable it abroad entirely. It looks free at home and behaves differently abroad, so verify with your carrier before depending on it. Enable it before departure — on iPhone under *Settings → Cellular →* your home line → *Wi-Fi Calling*, on Android under that SIM's settings on most skins — and place one test call while still on home soil. One safety note: emergency calls may route differently with Wi-Fi Calling active, so check how your carrier handles them where you're headed.

## Data roaming settings explained

The two roaming toggles do different jobs and get confused constantly:

- **Data Roaming (eSIM line): on abroad.** The eSIM's partner networks are foreign networks; without this toggle it cannot use them at all.
- **Data Roaming (home line): off, always, abroad.** Your home carrier's roaming rates apply the moment the home line uses foreign data. Off means off — calls and incoming SMS still work.

### The automatic network selection trap

**Automatic** network selection can park a line on the wrong partner. If the eSIM connects but crawls, switch its selection to manual and pick from the partner list on the plan page. The same manual trick controls the home line's voice rates — roaming off stops data, but a call you take still bills at the partner's voice rate, and partners of the same carrier can differ widely.

## Three bill shock scenarios and the one toggle that stops each

Every bill-shock story ends with a toggle that was already on the phone. Three of the most common:

**The silent connect.** The plane lands, the home line auto-registers on a foreign partner of your home carrier, and a background app refresh runs before you reach the gate. The bill arrives next month. *Prevented by* Data Roaming **off** on the home line — a line with roaming disabled can register for calls but cannot move a byte of foreign data, so there is nothing to bill.

**The wrong partner.** Everything is configured correctly, but automatic selection parks the home line on a partner your carrier bills at its top rate, and a five-minute call costs what a dinner should. *Prevented by* setting the home line's network selection to **manual** and choosing the partner your carrier lists as preferred, right after landing.

**The leftover data line.** Mid-trip the eSIM runs dry, you flip cellular data to the home line to check one thing, and forget for two days. *Prevented by* the **data line selection itself** — treat data-on-the-eSIM as a checked state, and when the eSIM runs out, top it up or buy the next plan rather than moving every megabyte onto roaming rates.

## Every toggle to set before you fly

Every dual SIM setting worth checking before you fly fits in one table:

| Toggle | Line | Set to | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data Roaming | eSIM line | On | lets the eSIM use partner networks |
| Data Roaming | home line | Off | blocks silent foreign data charges |
| Cellular data | either | eSIM | one line carries all data abroad |
| Network selection | home line | Manual | picks the cheaper roaming partner |
| Default voice line | either | home line | outbound calls bill predictably |

Thirty seconds in settings, once, before the airport — and every scenario above is already dead.

## Dual SIM battery and speed facts

Two registered lines cost a little standby battery — a few percent a day on modern hardware, rarely noticeable next to travel-day screen time. Speed is unaffected: the eSIM's data runs on the same local networks at the same speeds a local SIM would get, and 5G anchors to the data line on most hardware, so the eSIM gets full local speeds wherever networks offer it. If you want zero home-line drain, toggle the home SIM off entirely for data-heavy days and back on when you need its SMS — nothing is lost or reinstalled; it is a switch.
