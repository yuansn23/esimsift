Installing a travel eSIM takes about five minutes and works best *before* you leave home, on the Wi-Fi you trust. The exact screens differ between providers, but the flow is the same everywhere: buy a package, install the profile, leave it dormant until the plan's validity starts. One distinction solves most confusion: **installing** puts the profile on your phone, **activating** starts the clock — they are separate events.

## Two 30-second checks before you buy

1. **Confirm your phone supports eSIM.** Dial `*#06#` — if an EID number appears alongside the IMEI, the hardware is there. (Full instructions, including which models qualify: [eSIM compatibility check](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/).)
2. **Confirm it isn't carrier-locked.** A phone still locked to its original carrier refuses third-party eSIM profiles. If your contract device is paid off, the unlock is usually a free request to that carrier.

A third check decides *how* the install will go: five of the eight providers we track install through their own app, the other three from a plain QR code or website. The live table below shows which is which — [Airalo](/esim-providers/airalo/), [Holafly](/esim-providers/holafly/) and [Roamic](/esim-providers/roamic/) install directly; app-based brands are best installed at home, before the app-store login becomes a problem.

## How to install an eSIM on iPhone

1. Buy the package; the provider emails a QR code (or its app offers a direct install).
2. Open **Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM** (older iOS: *Cellular Plans*).
3. Scan the QR code, or approve the provider-app install prompt.
4. Choose **Secondary** for the new line and keep your home line as Primary.
5. On the final screen pick **Use Secondary for Cellular Data** — then turn **Data Roaming ON for the eSIM line only**. This is the step most people miss; without it, the eSIM can't register on local networks abroad.

The profile sits dormant until the plan's validity starts, so installing a week early is safe. How the two lines coexist — calls on one, data on the other — is covered in the [dual SIM and eSIM guide](/guides/dual-sim-and-esim/).

## How to install an eSIM on Android

1. Open **Settings → Network & internet → SIMs** (or *Connections → SIM manager* on Samsung).
2. Tap **Add eSIM / Download a SIM**, then scan the provider's QR code.
3. Confirm the download on Wi-Fi; the profile appears as a second SIM.
4. Set **Mobile data** to the new eSIM and enable its **Data Roaming** toggle.
5. If the provider lists APN settings, add exactly the APN string shown — one field, nothing more.

## Do you install an eSIM before or after you fly

Before — with one caveat about *when it activates*. Most providers start the validity clock when the eSIM first connects to a network in the destination, which is what you want: install on Sunday, land Thursday, the plan starts Thursday. A minority start the clock at installation or first use anywhere, so a 7-day package installed a week early could be half-expired before you fly. The plan's terms on its provider page state the trigger — match the validity window to your actual trip length, and our country pages sort every plan by length so the right one surfaces fast: see [Japan](/compare/japan/) or [Portugal](/compare/portugal/) for examples.

## What to do if an eSIM won't activate

- **"eSIM cannot be activated"** — usually a carrier-locked phone, or a QR profile already scanned once (they're mostly single-use; the provider must reissue).
- **No data after landing** — Data Roaming off on the eSIM line, or the phone still routing data through the home SIM. Check both toggles before anything else.
- **Full bars but nothing loads** — missing APN on Android, or slow partner-network registration; ten seconds of airplane mode forces a fresh attach.
- **Wrong country package** — don't let it connect. Contact the provider before validity starts; most will swap or refund an unused plan (each brand's policy is summarized in its [provider review](/esim-providers/)).
- **Half-installed profile** — a download interrupted mid-install; delete the partial profile and ask the provider to reissue rather than rescanning the dead QR code.

## Two habits that prevent 90% of problems

Install on home Wi-Fi with the phone charged, and screenshot the confirmation screen. Then, when you land: airplane mode on for ten seconds, off, wait a minute — the eSIM registers on a local partner network and the plan starts on its own. Still choosing a package?
