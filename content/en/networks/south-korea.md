## Why Korean mobile speeds look like a typo

South Korea posts the largest network numbers anywhere in our network map, and the margin is not marginal. Opensignal measured KT's 5G download at 486.2 Mbps in December 2025. Ookla's controlled drive testing across the Seoul to Incheon area recorded a median download of 853.37 Mbps on the leading carrier, with all three operators scoring between 982 and 993 out of 1000 in the same test.

For context, the equivalent 5G figures elsewhere on this site land in the 100 to 280 Mbps range. Korea operates in a different bracket, and that changes what a data plan is actually for. A 20 GB allowance on a Korean network goes further than the same allowance almost anywhere else, because the capacity to deliver it is there.

That is worth knowing before you buy, since it means the cheapest plan with the smallest allowance is often enough. The question is not whether the network can keep up. It is how much data your apps will get through, and on Korean infrastructure the answer is usually less than you expect.

## What tourists can and cannot buy on a Korean SIM

Korea enforces real-name registration on every mobile line, so identity requirements are strict by international standards. The rules sit with the [Ministry of Science and ICT](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/), and where they land for you depends entirely on which product you are buying.

| | Travel eSIM | Prepaid SIM in Korea | Korean postpaid contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registration | Provider's own, not yours | Real name with passport | Real name with residence card |
| Where you get it | Online, before you fly | Incheon Airport counters, carrier stores | Carrier stores only |
| Documentation | None | Passport, no ARC needed | Alien Registration Card plus a Korean payment method |
| Typical price range | Set by the provider | Roughly 30,000 to 60,000 won depending on duration | Monthly billing |
| Local phone number | Data-only in most cases | Yes | Yes |
| Best for | Almost every short trip | Longer stays needing a Korean number | Residents |

The middle column is the practical option for a visitor who wants a Korean number, and the airport counters make it reasonably painless. What is no longer possible is signing up for a monthly postpaid contract with a passport alone — that requires an Alien Registration Card and a Korean payment method, which puts it out of reach for a tourist.

Most travel eSIMs for Korea are data-only, and even the voice-capable tourist products cannot be used for Korean identity verification. If your trip depends on being reachable on a Korean number, buy at the airport. If it depends on data, a travel eSIM is simpler and cheaper.

## Where SK Telecom KT and LG U+ each have the edge

Opensignal and Ookla disagree about South Korea in a way that is genuinely informative rather than contradictory.

### SK Telecom is the best overall Korean network

Opensignal's December 2025 report gave SK Telecom nine awards outright plus three shared and the Best Network title, with Download Speed at 189.3 Mbps — about 35 Mbps clear of KT — and Coverage Experience at 9.4 out of 10, the highest of the three. It is the pick for anywhere outside the Seoul metropolitan area.

### KT holds the Korean 5G speed record

KT's 5G Download Speed award came with a figure of 486.2 Mbps, the highest on any page in this network map. In the Seoul to Incheon drive test it placed second overall, and KT is also the carrier most associated with foreigner-friendly plan products.

### LG U+ is the Korean 5G availability leader

LG U+ reached 90.3% availability, meaning users with a 5G device detect a 5G signal more of the time than on either rival. It also topped RootMetrics' Overall RootScore for the Seoul to Incheon area at 993 out of 1000, with the highest median download speed in the same test. It is the smallest of the three nationally and often the cheapest.

## Seoul against the rest of Korea

Korea is small, dense and highly urbanised, which compresses the coverage problem compared with the USA or Canada. Even so, the three networks separate in the places a visitor is most likely to go.

| | What to expect | Which network |
|---|---|---|
| Seoul and the metro, including subway tunnels | Near-total 5G coverage on all three | Any |
| Busan, Incheon, Daegu, Daejeon | Fast 5G throughout | Any |
| Jeju Island | Well covered around the coast and the main routes | SK Telecom or KT |
| The eastern coast and the mountain regions | Coverage thins away from the towns | SK Telecom |
| The Demilitarized Zone and border areas | Restricted and unreliable by nature | Do not rely on it |

SK Telecom's Coverage Experience score of 9.4 out of 10 is the reason it stays the safe answer away from the cities, and its lead in the Seoul subway is a smaller but real advantage if you are spending a week in the capital.

Korea's 2G networks closed years ago, in 2012 and 2020 depending on the carrier. There is no visitor-facing shutdown coming that you need to plan around. Bring a 4G or 5G handset with VoLTE support and the [is your phone compatible](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) will confirm the model. An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card you insert, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), and it runs on whatever bands your handset covers.

## Sizing a South Korea eSIM data plan

All three Korean carriers we track list 5G, so there is no 4G-only option and 5G is not a reason to pay more. What is unusual is how much the technology delivers — KT's 486.2 Mbps and the 853.37 Mbps median recorded in the Seoul to Incheon test are in a different class from the 100 to 280 Mbps typical elsewhere, and that is why Korea is the one market on this site where a modest allowance usually suffices.

Practically, Korean data plans are generous in effect. Navigation, translation, streaming and photo uploads all run faster, and a mid-range plan over a normal holiday is unlikely to run out. If your trip continues to Japan, the carrier detail on that side is on our [Japan eSIM networks](/networks/japan/) page, and an Asia regional plan is usually the cheaper purchase than two single-country ones.

Two closing notes. Korean spectrum is licensed nationally and concentrated in mid-band frequencies, so a handset that covers those bands gets the full benefit of the network. There is also no regional roaming union covering Korea, so a home SIM used here bills at international rates and any fair-use condition on a plan is worth reading before you rely on it.
