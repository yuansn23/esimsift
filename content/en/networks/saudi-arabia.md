## Why Saudi Arabia is a 5G market with a biometrics queue

Saudi Arabia is a country where the mobile network is genuinely ahead of what most visitors expect and the paperwork is genuinely further behind. Both facts shape the purchase.

Start with the network. stc measured 5G Download Speed at 260.2 Mbps in Opensignal's February 2026 report and took the 5G Coverage award, and 5G is available across central Riyadh, the Jeddah waterfront and the Eastern Province business corridor. This is one of the densest 5G builds in the world by availability, and a standard tourist product accesses it automatically on a compatible handset.

Then the paperwork. Saudi law requires biometric registration for every local SIM, which means a passport plus a fingerprint scan taken at the counter, and with some operators the border entry number issued at immigration as well. There is no identity number requirement for visitors, because a tourist uses the passport and that entry number rather than a resident permit. Airport kiosks handle the whole process in around a quarter of an hour. All {{< count-providers >}} brands we track avoid it entirely, and our [Saudi Arabia plan ranking](/compare/saudi-arabia/) compares them on price.

## Registration and the border number worth writing down

The registration requirement is the strictest in our data, and it is worth understanding before you decide how to buy.

Every local SIM is registered in person. The counter takes the passport, records it and scans a fingerprint, so the process cannot be completed in advance, remotely or by someone else. Some operators also ask for the ten-digit border entry number that immigration issues on arrival or that appears on an electronic visa, which is worth photographing as soon as you have it.

The kiosks at the main airports handle this well: they operate around the clock, the staff are used to visitors, and the transaction is quick by the standards of a biometric process. The friction is the queue behind a full long-haul arrival rather than the process itself. If your trip is short, that queue is the entire argument for a travel eSIM.

There is a second consideration worth naming. Saudi Arabia has rules about who may enter the cities of Mecca and Medina, and those rules apply to visitors generally rather than to mobile networks. Coverage in those areas is strong and heavily reinforced, and it becomes congested during pilgrimage periods, so download offline material before entering them if your trip includes a visit.

## Which Saudi network to ride

The three networks are close in the cities and diverge outside them, so the useful question is how much desert your itinerary includes.

### stc leads the independent testing and the desert coverage

stc won Download Speed outright at 69.3 Mbps in Opensignal's February 2026 report, nine Mbps clear of Mobily, and took 5G Download Speed at 260.2 Mbps, Coverage at 8.4 out of 10 and the 5G Coverage award. It is also the network that holds up on the Riyadh to Jeddah corridor, the Red Sea projects and the roads around AlUla, which is where the other two thin out. For anything beyond a single-city trip this is the host to look for.

### Mobily wins the experience awards

Mobily lost the speed award and won most of the others: Video Experience at 64.5, Time on Network at 98.1%, Consistent Quality at 64.7% and Reliability at 839. Time on Network at 98.1% is a statement about how consistently you stay connected, and it is a strong figure for the second network. For a city trip in Riyadh or Jeddah it is a perfectly good host, and it usually prices below stc.

### Zain is the value option with a narrower footprint

Zain is the smallest of the three by market share and normally the cheapest way into Saudi data. Its measured profile sits a band below the other two on coverage, and the places where that shows are exactly the places people travel to Saudi Arabia for: the northern archaeological sites, the desert roads and the coast away from Jeddah.

Read the awards as a ranking and the speed bands as indicative ranges. One set comes from Opensignal's independent measurement programme, the ranges are our own compilation from carrier listings, and the specific mast you attach to still decides what you feel.

The registration picture is the most physical in our data. Saudi Arabia takes a biometric reading for every local line, so the process resists any remote shortcut and the visitor's alternative is a counter visit at the airport.

The eSIM picture is the usual regional one. Saudi carriers offer eSIM, and a visitor can request one at a kiosk, but the biometric requirement applies to the line rather than to the format, so the digital part is the profile rather than the paperwork. That pushes the tourist route through an international provider.

## 5G in Saudi Arabia and where the map still runs out

All three networks run commercial 5G and every Saudi carrier profile we track lists it, with stc's measured 260.2 Mbps the headline figure and 5G available well beyond the three biggest cities. By availability this is one of the most advanced builds in the world, and the limit is geography rather than technology.

The gaps look like the map. The desert roads between cities, the far north towards the Jordanian border, the Empty Quarter and the interior away from the main corridors all leave long stretches without coverage, and AlUla and the northern archaeological sites are where the choice of host network is felt most. Download offline maps before any desert route, and carry more water than you think you need if you are driving one.
