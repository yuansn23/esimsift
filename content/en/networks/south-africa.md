## Why South Africa's registration rule changes the decision

South Africa is the one destination on this site where the registration rule follows you into the eSIM. Everywhere else, the argument for a travel eSIM is convenience: no queue, no paperwork, working before you land. Here that argument becomes the whole case, because the local alternative does not skip the paperwork even when it is digital.

RICA requires every SIM to be registered against an identifiable person before it can be used, and the rule covers prepaid, contract, physical SIMs and eSIMs sold by South African networks. A locally bought eSIM is RICA-registered like a plastic card. An international travel eSIM is not, because it is provisioned outside the country against your home subscription.

That is worth weighing against the price. South African data is not expensive, and a local prepaid bundle gives you a local number and better rates. What it does not give you is a way to arrive with a working line.

## The RICA rule and what a visitor actually needs

RICA is set out in the Regulation of Interception of Communications and Provision of Communication-Related Information Act, and it is administered by the networks under the oversight of [ICASA](https://www.icasa.org.za/), the sector regulator.

For a visitor the requirement is an identity document, and a valid passport is the one that applies. Proof of address may also be requested, depending on the operator and the registration type, and outlets differ in what they currently accept. Registration is free and takes fifteen to thirty minutes in a store. Bring the passport itself: a photograph of it is not a document.

The practical consequence is a sequencing problem. Arrive late, or with an onward transfer, and the store may be shut, which leaves you without a local number until the next morning. A travel eSIM installed before you fly removes that risk entirely, and it is the reason most visitors to South Africa never see RICA at all.

## Which South African network to ride

The South African split is the clearest on this site, because the two leaders win different things.

### MTN leads on measured South African speed

Opensignal's August 2025 report put MTN top on Download Speed outright at 60.1 Mbps, an 18 Mbps lead over second-placed Vodacom, and MTN collected 11 of the 15 awards on offer. For a trip weighted toward Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban, it is the faster network.

### Vodacom is the one for the reserves and the long roads

Vodacom is the network that holds furthest from the cities, and it is the one most often named for the safari regions, the national parks and the Garden Route backcountry. On a self-drive itinerary the reach matters more than the measured median, because the alternative to a usable signal is no signal at all.

### Telkom is the budget city option

Telkom is the cheapest of the three and is listed as 4G rather than 5G in our carrier data. It is fine for a city stay and the wrong choice for a long rural route, where its coverage is the thinnest of the three.

Read the two columns as separate verdicts rather than one combined score. One is built from crowdsourced user readings and the other from the median of tests people ran voluntarily, so they can rank the same networks differently without either being wrong. South Africa also runs more operators than the three we track, so treat this table as the networks your travel eSIM actually uses rather than as the whole market.

## South African data rules and how the local route works

Two South African rules shape how you buy and register data, and the first one is unusual for an eSIM.

| | Travel eSIM | South African prepaid SIM | South African eSIM |
|---|---|---|---|
| RICA registration | Not required | Yes, passport plus possible address proof | Yes, same rule as plastic |
| Local number | No | Yes | Yes |
| Works across the region | Only with a multi-country plan | No, charged as roaming abroad | No, charged as roaming abroad |
| Registration time | None | 15 to 30 minutes in store | 15 to 30 minutes in store |
| Best for | Almost every short trip | Longer stays wanting a South African number | Residents and long stays |

The registration picture is the strictest on this site and the only one where the eSIM route is not automatically faster. Colombian law registers prepaid lines against a passport, Croatian law asks for a passport and nothing else, and South African law asks for an identity document that can be a passport and then may ask for an address as well, depending on the operator and the product.

The pricing picture is the other way round. South African prepaid bundles are competitive, the networks sell tourist packs with a month of validity, and the local route wins on price for a stay of a fortnight or more. The trade is a store visit, an identity document and a possible second document, weighted against a plan that works when you land.

## 5G in South Africa and how to plan around the distances

All three networks in our data list 5G, and every South African carrier profile we track records it. What is worth knowing is where it is. 5G sits in parts of Johannesburg and Cape Town and on the dense metro routes between them, while 4G LTE remains the standard across the main tourist routes and most towns.

The distances are what make South African coverage different from a European destination. A drive from Johannesburg to the Kruger National Park, or from Cape Town along the Garden Route, crosses long stretches where the network you picked is the difference between intermittent signal and none. The Kgalagadi, the deep Drakensberg and parts of the Wild Coast have no coverage on any network, and no plan choice changes that.

An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card it accepts, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), so it works with whatever bands your handset covers. The [phone compatibility checker](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) covers specific models.
