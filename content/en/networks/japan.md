## Does the carrier behind your Japan eSIM change your coverage

Not in Japan, and this is the most useful fact on the page. The market runs on three national carrier networks — NTT Docomo, SoftBank and KDDI — and all {{< count-providers >}} brands we track (Airalo, aloSIM, Holafly, Roami, Roamic, Saily, Ubigi and Yesim) list all three as hosts. The brand-by-brand table below shows it row after row.

The consequence is that no travel eSIM brand can sell you better coverage in Japan. A cheaper Japan eSIM is not a network downgrade here, and that is not true everywhere — in plenty of destinations a budget plan rides one carrier and pays for it in rural signal. In Japan that trade-off does not exist, so the variables that actually matter are price per gigabyte, the shape of the data plan, hotspot rules, validity and support.

That reframes the buying question. Instead of hunting for the Japan eSIM with the best network, ask which brand sells the data plan your trip needs at the lowest price — and the answer moves with trip length, which is why our [Japan plan ranking](/compare/japan/) sorts all 177 plans by $/GB and $/day rather than by carrier.

## Where each Japanese 5G network is best on speed and reach

Japan's three national 5G networks are near-identical in availability and clearly different in performance. Independent testing splits them by what you measure.

### NTT Docomo owns Japanese reach

In Opensignal's April 2026 Japan report Docomo took Coverage at 9.0 out of 10 and won 5G Download Speed at 159.1 Mbps. If your itinerary leaves the cities for the mountains or the islands of Okinawa, Docomo's footprint is the reason to care what sits under your plan.

### SoftBank owns Japanese 5G peak speed

Opensignal measured SoftBank fastest on download at 65.1 Mbps, and Ookla named it the fastest mobile network in Japan for the first half of 2026, with a median download of 73.8 Mbps ahead of au's 67.9 and a median 5G download of 153.6 Mbps. That strength concentrates in cities and along the corridors around major stations.

### KDDI au is the Japanese balance point

KDDI, sold locally as au, collected the most awards of any operator in the April 2026 cycle — ten outright wins plus one shared. Nationwide reach sits close to Docomo but leans west, with a particularly firm grip on coastal routes.

A caution if you compare these figures elsewhere. The panels are assembled from unlike evidence, and placing them head to head produces a number that means nothing. Treat each panel as its own ranking of the same three Japanese carriers.

## Is 5G available on a Japan eSIM

Yes, and in Japan the 5G question is close to settled. All three national carriers run commercial 5G and every carrier profile we track lists it, so a Japan travel eSIM is not quietly capped at 4G. Independent testing puts the technology where you would expect in a market this dense: Opensignal measured Docomo's 5G download at 159.1 Mbps, and Ookla recorded a median 5G download of 153.6 Mbps for SoftBank in the same cycle.

For a buyer that means 5G is not a premium worth paying extra for, because there is no carrier without it. The useful checks are narrower — does the plan in front of you list 5G, does your handset support the Japanese bands (the [handset compatibility guide](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) settles that in 30 seconds), and does the plan's fair-use policy throttle speed after a daily allowance rather than the network itself. An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card you insert, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), so the plan travels with the device rather than the SIM tray.

Three ways to get data in Japan, and the choice turns on rules and logistics more than on price. The general trade-offs live in our [eSIM vs physical SIM guide](/guides/esim-vs-physical-sim/); what follows is what is specific to Japan.

## Japan eSIM rules and roaming basics for tourists

Two Japan-specific rules decide more purchases than any speed test, plus one billing trap worth understanding before you fly. The passport requirement for local prepaid cards is set by the [Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications](https://www.soumu.go.jp/english/), which regulates Japanese telecoms.

### Why a Japan prepaid SIM needs a passport

Japanese law requires SoftBank, Docomo and KDDI to register a local prepaid SIM against your passport. Travel eSIMs sit outside that regime — buy online, scan the code, connect, with no document upload at any step.

### Instant activation against a SoftBank counter SIM

SoftBank's own tourist card activates only between 09:00 and 21:00 JST. A travel eSIM has no such window, which matters most on a late arrival into Narita or Haneda when the counters are shut and you want data before the train.

### No roaming union covers Japan for tourists

Japan has nothing like the EU's free-roaming arrangement for inbound visitors, so a home SIM used here bills at international rates. That is the whole case for a prepaid Japan data plan: a travel eSIM is bought up front at a posted price, with no roaming surcharge and no bill to open when you get home.

Before you pay, run four checks. The plan lists all three carriers. It lists 5G if you care. Hotspot sharing is allowed if you need it. And the fair-use terms are on the page rather than in a support ticket. Live discounts sit on the [eSIM promo codes](/esim-deals/), and the [data calculator](/tools/) will size an allowance to your dates.

Two closing notes. Japanese spectrum is licensed nationally across bands that some imported handsets do not tune, so confirm your device before you rely on 5G. Japan's carriers have also retired their older networks, which means a handset needs VoLTE support rather than 2G or 3G fallback to register at all.
