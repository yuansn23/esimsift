## How international roaming changes what a China eSIM can reach

China is the one destination in this network map where the question is routing rather than signal. This one is about routing, and routing matters more.

A travel eSIM is issued by a company outside mainland China. When you use it, your data session is carried back to that provider's home network and reaches the internet from outside the mainland rather than through a domestic Chinese carrier. A SIM card bought inside China is a domestic line, and domestic mobile traffic stays on mainland networks. The regulator for the domestic market is the [Ministry of Industry and Information Technology](https://www.miit.gov.cn/).

The difference shows up immediately on a normal trip. Services that are unreachable on a domestic Chinese connection generally work on an inbound travel eSIM with no additional software, no configuration and nothing to install after you land. That is not a feature the provider advertises as a workaround — it is simply how international roaming has always worked — but for a visitor it is the single most consequential fact about buying data in China.

It also means the comparison is not between three Chinese carriers at all. It is between a travel eSIM and a local SIM, and the two give you access to different sets of services.

## Install your China eSIM before you fly

Skipping the pre-flight install is the failure mode that ruins China trips, and it is entirely avoidable.

Provider websites, app stores and activation portals are frequently unreachable from a mainland connection. If you land, buy a local SIM, and then try to install a travel eSIM as a backup, you may find you cannot reach the provider to do it. The same applies to anyone who assumes they will sort an eSIM out at the airport on hotel wifi that is itself on a mainland connection.

The fix takes two minutes at home: install the eSIM before departure, while you are on a connection you control, and simply switch the line on when you land. An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card you insert — the standard is maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/) — which is exactly why it can be installed from anywhere. Our [installation guide](/guides/how-to-install-esim/) walks through it. Do it before you pack rather than after you arrive.

## Will a China eSIM work in a phone bought in China

The domestic-only eSIM rule catches a specific group of travellers, and it is worth checking before you buy anything.

| Phone | eSIM support | Travel eSIM from abroad |
|---|---|---|
| iPhone bought outside mainland China | Yes, multiple profiles | Works |
| iPhone bought inside mainland China | Domestic profiles only | Does not activate |
| Most Android phones bought in China | Domestic profiles only, if any | Does not activate |
| Any phone, with a physical SIM slot | Not applicable | Use a physical travel SIM instead |

Mainland China only authorised eSIM in phones in October 2025, and the implementation is domestic-first: handsets sold there support Chinese carrier profiles, and the international eSIM-only models carry more profile slots than the same hardware sold locally.

If you are travelling with a phone bought in China, a physical travel SIM is the workable option. If your phone came from anywhere else and supports eSIM, the eSIM route is straightforward.

## China Mobile China Unicom and China Telecom compared

China is the one major market with no Opensignal Mobile Network Experience report, so there is no independent crowdsourced per-carrier ranking to set beside the other pages here. Ookla's Speedtest Global Index tracks the country month by month and put mainland China's median mobile download at 221.77 Mbps in August 2026, which places it in the global top tier without telling you which carrier to prefer.

What is well established is the shape of the market.

### China Mobile has the widest 5G reach

China Mobile carries the largest subscriber base and the broadest footprint, including subway tunnels, smaller cities and most tourist routes. In a country this large, reach matters more than peak speed, and it is the network to be on if your itinerary goes beyond the big coastal cities.

### China Unicom is fast in the cities

China Unicom is a frequent host for inbound travel eSIMs and its rural reach sits a step behind China Mobile.

### China Telecom is strong in the south

China Telecom is strong in every major city and is a solid second choice anywhere.

## Coverage from the coastal cities to the interior

China's scale makes coverage planning genuinely necessary, and the distances are larger than most visitors expect.

| | What to expect | Which network |
|---|---|---|
| Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen | Very fast 5G throughout, including the subway | Any |
| Major tourist cities and the coastal strip | Reliable 5G and 4G | Any |
| High-speed rail corridors | Coverage along the route with occasional gaps | China Mobile |
| Smaller cities and county towns | Solid 4G, thinner 5G | China Mobile |
| Inland provinces far from the coast | Coverage follows settlements rather than the map | China Mobile, and expect gaps |

China Mobile operates across the widest area of the three, which is why it stays the safe answer once a trip leaves the coastal cities. Because every provider we track offers it, that is a check on the plan rather than on the brand.

China is also retiring older technologies. China Mobile's 2G authorisation lapsed at the end of 2025 and provincial 3G shutdowns have begun through 2026, so a 4G or 5G handset with VoLTE is the right device to bring. ## Four checks before you buy a China eSIM data plan

Four checks cover almost everything that can go wrong on a China trip.

First, does the plan route internationally rather than reselling a domestic Chinese line. This is the whole point, and any reputable travel eSIM does it by default. Second, is your phone one that supports inbound eSIM profiles, because a handset bought in China will not. Third, have you installed it before departure. Fourth, does the allowance match the trip length, since map apps, translation tools and photo uploads all consume differently when you are also reaching services that are unavailable locally.

City networks can also suffer congestion at peak commuter times, particularly on the metro. And prepaid plans bought locally carry fair-use conditions that are rarely explained in English, which is another reason the inbound eSIM route is simpler.
