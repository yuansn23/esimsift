## Why Morocco is a coverage question rather than a speed question

Morocco has three national networks, commercial 5G on all of them since late 2025, and mobile data that costs little. On paper there is nothing to choose between the operators. In practice there is one thing, and it is not speed.

It is reach. Maroc Telecom's advantage in the independent testing is not that it is faster in Casablanca, it is that it keeps working in places where the other two do not. The Atlas passes, the trekking trails around Imlil and Toubkal, the gorges on the road to the desert and the desert edge itself are where a Moroccan itinerary usually ends up, and they are where the host network on your plan stops being an abstraction.

That shapes the buying decision. Inside the cities, price decides. Outside them, the host network decides, and it is worth knowing which one your plan lists before you commit to a southern route. ## Registration and what the seller does with your passport

The second thing to understand is the registration step, and Morocco applies it without exceptions.

Every prepaid line has to be registered to a passport, and the seller records or copies the document at the point of sale. This is true at the airport, at a carrier shop in Casablanca and at a small phone shop down a medina alley, so there is no counter where the step is skipped. It is a legal requirement rather than a shop policy, and it is enforced at the moment of purchase.

For a visitor that produces the usual trade-off with an unusual shape. The local SIM is cheap per gigabyte and costs you a shop visit. A travel eSIM bought before you fly is live on landing and needs no document at any point.

One detail worth knowing if you buy locally: the copy of your passport stays with the seller and the registration is tied to the number. If you buy a second SIM later you repeat the process, so it is worth buying the allowance you actually need rather than topping up a series of small purchases.

## Which Moroccan network to ride

The three networks are close inside the cities and diverge sharply outside them, so the useful question is where your itinerary goes.

### Maroc Telecom covers the Atlas and the desert edge

Maroc Telecom was named Best Network in Opensignal's April 2026 report with seven sole wins, leading the overall download and upload speed awards, Coverage, 5G Coverage and Consistent Quality. Seven sole wins is the widest margin of any operator in our Moroccan data, and the coverage awards are the ones that matter for a road trip: the Atlas passes, the trekking valleys and the roads towards Merzouga are where it holds on.

### Orange Maroc is the urban network

Orange Maroc is a solid city network and its measured profile sits a band below Maroc Telecom on coverage and speed. For a trip that stays in Casablanca, Rabat and Marrakesh it is entirely adequate, and it is the correct host for a short coastal break. Turning inland towards the mountains is where the gap opens.

### Inwi is the cheapest and the most limited

Inwi is usually the cheapest way into Moroccan data, and its coverage reputation stops around Marrakesh. That is not a small caveat in a country whose best trips go south, so an Inwi-hosted plan is worth having only if your itinerary stays on the coast and in the imperial cities.

Read the awards as a ranking and the speed bands as indicative ranges. One set comes from Opensignal's independent measurement programme, the ranges are our own compilation from carrier listings, and the specific mast you attach to still decides what you feel.

## Morocco prepaid rules and why the local eSIM route is awkward

Two Moroccan rules shape how you buy data, and the second one is unusual for the region.

| | Travel eSIM | Moroccan prepaid SIM | Moroccan carrier eSIM |
|---|---|---|---|
| ID required | None | Passport, recorded at the point of sale | Passport, recorded in store |
| Where the registration happens | Before you fly | At any seller, including small shops | In store |
| Live before you land | Yes | No | No |
| Best for | Almost every short trip | Longer stays wanting a local number | Residents |

The registration picture is strict and universal. Morocco registers prepaid lines against a passport wherever they are sold, so a visitor needs the physical document and should not expect to be waved through at a small shop.

The eSIM picture is the usual regional one. Moroccan carriers do offer eSIM, but the flows assume local registration, which means a visitor cannot activate one remotely. For a tourist that pushes the eSIM route through an international travel eSIM provider, and it is why the brand comparison on this page is settled on price.

## 5G in Morocco and the four places data runs out

All three networks run commercial 5G and every Moroccan carrier profile we track lists it. Morocco is unusual in that the three launched 5G on the same day in November 2025, so there was no year-long window where one operator had it and the others did not. Availability across the major cities is now broad, and the coverage award that Maroc Telecom took is the part that decides whether 5G reaches you further out.

The gaps are geographic. The High Atlas passes and the trekking valleys around Toubkal, the Middle Atlas away from the main roads, the gorges and the approach roads to Merzouga and the desert camps, and the deep interior of the old medinas all thin out or stop. Download offline maps before any of those, and treat the medina alleys as a no-signal zone regardless of which network you are on.

An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card it accepts, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), so it works with whatever bands your handset covers.
