## Why registration is the hard part in Colombia

Colombia is not a coverage problem. The country has three national networks with dense 5G in the cities, mobile data is among the cheapest in the Americas, and every travel eSIM brand we track connects without difficulty. The friction sits somewhere else entirely: on the paperwork attached to a local line.

That changes the shape of the buying decision. There is no meaningful "which network reaches my hotel" question for a trip that stays in Bogota, Medellin or Cartagena. The variables are price, the host network your plan rides, and a set of registration rules that make the local SIM route slower here than almost anywhere else in the region.

The regulator is the [CRC](https://www.crcom.gov.co/), which publishes the access statistics and the measured quality results quoted on this page. All {{< count-providers >}} travel eSIM brands we track connect to the same three networks — Claro, Movistar and Tigo — which the brand table below shows row by row. No provider can sell you better Colombian coverage than another, so the brand decides the bill and nothing else. Our [comparison of Colombia eSIM plans](/compare/colombia/) ranks them on that basis.

## The IMEI rule that decides whether you need a local SIM

Colombia has a device rule that most visitors never encounter, and it is worth thirty seconds because it changes the arithmetic on a longer stay.

The CRC runs a handset registration regime. A phone that attaches to a Colombian operator as a local subscriber has to have its IMEI registered, and operators send warning messages in the run-up to the deadline. A handset in international roaming is exempt. A travel eSIM keeps you in the second category, so the rule never reaches you.

Two details matter if you plan to buy local. The block applies to the device rather than to the SIM, so changing the card does not restore service. And the exemption attaches to roaming, not to the phone, so the same handset can be outside the rule this month and inside it next month once a Colombian line is active. Check the current position before you commit to a local number.

## Which Colombian network to ride

The Colombian split is less tidy than the Dutch one, because the three networks differ more outside the cities than inside them.

### Claro is the widest Colombian network

The CRC measured Claro highest on average 4G download speed at 20.2 Mbps, and it is the network that keeps working through the coffee region, the smaller Caribbean towns and the intercity roads where the other two thin out. If your itinerary leaves the three big cities, Claro is the safer foundation.

### Movistar competes on urban speed

Movistar is strong in Bogota and Medellin and its measured numbers put it in the same band as Claro inside the cities. The gap opens on the road. It is a good pick for a city-only trip and a weaker one for a self-drive route.

### Tigo is the budget middle

Tigo is dependable in the cities and usually the cheapest of the three. Its measured 4G quality sits below Claro's, and its rural coverage is the thinnest, so it suits a trip that stays in Bogota, Medellin and Cartagena rather than one that crosses the country.

Read the figures as indicative bands rather than as a ranking you can bank on. One set comes from the regulator's own measurement programme, and the congestion on the specific mast you attach to still decides what you feel. Neither number tells you how fast a travel eSIM will run for you.

## Colombian prepaid rules and why the local eSIM route is awkward

Two Colombian rules shape how you actually buy and register data, and the second one is unusual.

The registration picture is stricter than in Croatia or the Netherlands, and it is a legal requirement rather than a store policy. Colombia registers prepaid lines against an identity document, so a visitor needs the physical passport at the counter and should not expect a photo on a phone to be accepted.

The eSIM picture is the opposite of the usual one. Colombian carriers do offer eSIM, but Claro sells it on postpaid only and requires a visit, while Tigo and Movistar build their prepaid eSIM flows around Colombian identity documents. For a visitor that effectively means the eSIM route runs through an international travel eSIM provider rather than a local network, which is the reverse of the advice that works in Thailand or Turkey.

## 5G in Colombia and what to plan for outside the cities

All three networks run commercial 5G, and every Colombian carrier profile we track lists it. What is worth knowing is how concentrated it is. 5G reached 12.4% of mobile internet accesses at the end of 2025 and was deployed on 3.5 GHz capacity spectrum, because the 700 MHz low-band lot went unsold at the December 2023 auction. Capacity spectrum carries a lot of data over a short distance, which is exactly the shape that serves a dense city centre and leaves a long road on 4G.

So plan around 4G and treat 5G as a city bonus. Where the gaps are is predictable once you picture the map: the Amazon basin outside Leticia, the Pacific coast around Nuqui and Bahia Solano, the beaches inside Tayrona National Park, and the multi-day Ciudad Perdida trek, which has no coverage for the whole walk. Download offline maps before any of those, and switch off automatic network selection near the Venezuelan and Ecuadorian borders if you would rather not pay for an accidental attachment.
