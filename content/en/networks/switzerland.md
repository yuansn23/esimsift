## Why Switzerland is the one European country where roaming does not save you

Switzerland sits in the middle of Europe and outside the European Union, and that single fact reorganises the entire buying decision.

The Union's roam-like-at-home rules do not apply here, which means there is no price cap on what a European operator can charge you for using your own plan in Switzerland. Some operators include the country and many do not, so whether your existing SIM is usable depends on your tariff rather than on a rule. Travellers who assume Switzerland behaves like France or Germany are the ones who come home to a large bill.

That is why a Switzerland-specific plan is worth considering even for a short trip, and why the usual advice about European destinations does not transfer. ## Registration and the counter visit that follows

The second thing to know is the registration requirement, and it is a legal obligation rather than a shop policy.

Swiss law requires every prepaid SIM to be registered against a passport or identity document at the point of sale, and that applies to tourist products as much as to local ones. The document is recorded at the counter, so bring the physical one. Carriers also offer eSIM with digital identity verification, but the flow is still built around Swiss documentation, which leaves it awkward for a visitor.

Where you complete the purchase changes the experience more than in most countries. Zurich airport has official shops from all three networks inside the terminal, at the same prices as their city stores. Geneva airport does not: the terminal has resellers rather than carrier shops, and the starter packs cost noticeably more than the same product bought in town. If you land at Geneva, the honest advice is to wait.

## Which Swiss network to ride

The three Swiss networks diverge less in the cities than in the mountains, and the awards reflect that split.

### Swisscom wins almost everything and covers the Alps

Swisscom took the Best Network title in Opensignal's April 2026 Switzerland report with six awards: Coverage at 9.4 out of 10, 5G Coverage, Time on Network at 99.1%, Consistent Quality at 89.7%, Reliability at 954 and 5G Download Speed at 209.1 Mbps. Time on Network at 99.1% is a statement about how rarely you fall back to nothing, and the coverage awards are the ones that matter at altitude. Swisscom's alpine build reaches high mountain passes and remote valleys that the other two do not, which is what makes it the default host for a mountain itinerary.

### Sunrise takes the time on 5G award

Sunrise won Time on 5G at 43.7%, which is a different claim from fastest 5G: it describes how much of your connected time is spent on the newer network rather than on 4G. Its measured profile sits one band below Swisscom and its coverage reputation stops short of the highest ground. For a city trip or a rail itinerary it is a perfectly good host, and it is usually cheaper than Swisscom's.

### Salt is the budget network with the weakest alpine reach

Salt took 5G Upload Speed at 36.4 Mbps and is normally the cheapest way into Swiss data, occasionally with a free starter that requires a top-up. Its measured speed band is the lowest of the three, and its high-altitude coverage is the weakest, so Salt is the right host for Zurich, Geneva and Basel and the wrong one for a ski week.

Read the awards as a ranking and the speed bands as indicative ranges. One set comes from Opensignal's independent measurement programme, the ranges are our own compilation from carrier listings, and the specific mast you attach to still decides what you feel.

Two Swiss rules shape how you buy data, and the first one has no equivalent in the neighbouring countries.

| Where to buy | Online, before you fly | Zurich airport at city prices, Geneva at a premium | Carrier stores |
| Best for | Almost every short trip | Stays of a month or more | Residents |

The roaming position is the part that has no parallel nearby. Switzerland is outside the Union's roam-like-at-home framework, so neither a European SIM used here nor a Swiss SIM used in the Union enjoys a price cap. That cuts both ways, and it is worth checking your own tariff before deciding whether you need a Swiss plan at all.

The registration picture is strict by regional standards. Switzerland registers prepaid lines against a passport at the point of sale, so the counter visit is unavoidable for a local product.

## 5G in Switzerland and what mountain coverage really means

All three networks run commercial 5G and every Swiss carrier profile we track lists it, with Swisscom's measured 5G Download Speed of 209.1 Mbps the headline figure and Sunrise's 43.7% Time on 5G the broadest 5G footprint. The build is dense in the cities and along the rail corridors, which is where most of a Swiss itinerary sits.

Altitude is the variable. Coverage on Swisscom extends to high mountain passes, remote side valleys and places such as the Jungfraujoch, and on Sunrise and Salt it thins out above the tree line. For a hiking or ski trip that is not a detail: it decides whether a downloaded map is a backup or the only thing you have. Download offline maps before any high route regardless of the network on your plan.

An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card it accepts, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), so it works with whatever bands your handset covers. The [phone compatibility checker](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) covers specific models.
