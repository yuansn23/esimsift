## The two French rules that shape a SIM purchase

France is a mature market where the networks are good and broadly similar, so the decision tends to turn on regulation rather than on performance. Two rules matter, and the regulator behind both is [ARCEP](https://www.arcep.fr/).

### Why a French prepaid SIM needs a passport

Buying a French prepaid SIM means showing a passport at the point of sale and the carrier records it. There is no French address requirement and no waiting period, but there is a counter visit and a document. A travel eSIM bought before you fly has no such step.

### EU no roaming rules attach to the French SIM

European Union roaming attaches to the SIM rather than to the plan. A French SIM bought locally keeps working across the EU and EEA at domestic rates, subject to fair-use limits. A France-only travel eSIM does not — it is provisioned for French networks and stops at the border. That distinction decides whether you need a single-country plan or a Europe one.

## Where Orange Bouygues and SFR each have the edge

France's three national networks split unusually cleanly, and the split is between two different kinds of strength.

### Orange wins everything measurable on the French network

Opensignal's November 2025 report gave Orange Download Speed at 83.8 Mbps, 5G Download Speed at 273.4 Mbps ahead of SFR's 223.8, and Coverage Experience at 9.3 out of 10. Ookla separately made it the fastest mobile provider in France for the second half of 2024 at a 122.77 Mbps median download, with a median 5G download of 281.3 Mbps. If your trip includes rural France, the Atlantic coast or the Alps, Orange's reach is the reason to care what sits under your plan.

### Bouygues wins the French experience categories

Bouygues collected 12 of 17 awards in the same cycle, including Video Experience, Voice App Experience and Consistent Quality, and shares Reliability with Orange at a statistically tied 925 points. That is a network optimised for how it feels to use rather than for what it registers on a speed test — a meaningful distinction if you spend the trip on video calls and streaming rather than on downloads.

### SFR is the value middle option in France

SFR is the middle option with fast 5G bursts and a wide footprint that sits just behind Orange on coverage. It is often the cheaper of the three, which is why it appears on so many budget plans.

The two columns are built by different methods and are not interchangeable — one averages what real users experienced, the other takes the midpoint of speed tests people chose to run. That is enough to reorder the same three networks. Neither predicts your own connection.

## What France is doing with its older networks

France is midway through retiring the technologies that predate 4G, and the timeline is worth knowing if you travel with an older handset or a cellular tracker.

| | Status | What it affects |
|---|---|---|
| 2G | Orange retiring across mainland France during 2026, with pilot departments from March | Older feature phones, some trackers and telemetry devices |
| 3G | Orange shutting down from the end of 2028 | Devices that fall back to 3G for calls or data |
| 4G and 5G | Staying, and expanding | Any modern phone with VoLTE support |

The practical takeaway is simple. Bring a 4G or 5G handset that supports VoLTE and none of this touches you. Bring an older device and you may find it does not register on a French network at all. An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card you insert, and the standard behind it is maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), but the network technology a handset supports is fixed by its hardware. The [handset compatibility guide](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) resolves that before you pack.

## A France eSIM against a French SIM with no roaming charges

France offers three routes to mobile data, and the trade-offs are shaped by the registration rule.

| | Travel eSIM | French prepaid SIM | Roaming on a home SIM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity check | None | Passport, recorded at purchase | None |
| Works across the EU | Only with a Europe plan | Yes, subject to fair-use limits | Yes, if your home carrier allows it |
| When it works | On landing, any hour | After a counter visit | On landing |
| Cost for a week | Posted price, no surprises | Cheap per GB, plus the detour | Depends entirely on your home tariff |
| Main drawback | Data only, no French number | Counter queue and paperwork on arrival | Possible surcharge outside your allowance |

Visitors from the European Union should check their own tariff first, because roam-like-at-home may already cover France at no extra cost. Everyone else is choosing between a travel eSIM and a counter purchase, and for a week-long trip the eSIM usually wins on time even when the local SIM wins on pennies. ## What to verify on your phone before a French 5G trip

All three carriers we track run commercial 5G, and every French profile on this page lists it, so France has no 4G-only trap. Opensignal measured Orange's 5G download at 273.4 Mbps in November 2025 with SFR at 223.8 Mbps, which puts French 5G among the stronger networks in Western Europe.

That makes 5G a settled question rather than a selling point. The checks worth running are narrower. Does the plan in front of you list 5G. Does your handset cover the European bands. Can it make VoLTE calls, which matters as 3G is retired. And if your trip crosses borders, does the plan cover the countries on your route — our network pages for [Spain](/networks/spain/), [Germany](/networks/germany/) and [Italy](/compare/italy/) cover the carriers next door.

For a trip that stays inside France, the last thing to weigh is the shape of the plan. A short city break needs a small allowance over a few days; a driving holiday through Provence needs more, and the [data allowance calculator](/tools/) will estimate it from your apps and dates. Live discounts sit on the [live eSIM discounts](/esim-deals/).

Two practical notes before you buy. French spectrum is licensed nationally, and the bands in use differ from those in North America, so an imported handset is worth checking. Peak-hour congestion in Paris and Lyon affects all three networks equally, and upload throughput is the figure to watch if you plan to post while you travel.
