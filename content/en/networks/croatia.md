## Why Croatia is a coastline question rather than a city one

Most coverage questions are about whether a network reaches a place. Croatia's is about whether it reaches the water. Two networks cover the inhabited islands and the whole coast in 4G and increasingly 5G, the cities are unproblematic, and the practical gaps all sit in the same three places: the mountain interior, the deep bays of the outer islands, and the middle of a long ferry crossing.

That changes what you are buying. There is no meaningful "which network covers Split" question, and no network can sell you a reach the other does not have on the mainland. The variables are price, the host network your plan rides, and whether your trip spends enough time on the water for the island reach of Hrvatski Telekom to be worth paying for.

The regulator is [HAKOM](https://www.hakom.hr/), the Croatian Regulatory Authority for Network Industries, which publishes the market data behind the coverage claims on this page. All {{< count-providers >}} travel eSIM brands we track connect to the same Croatian networks — Hrvatski Telekom and A1 Hrvatska — which the brand table below shows row by row. Our [comparison of Croatia eSIM plans](/compare/croatia/) ranks them on price rather than on reach, because in Croatia that is the honest basis.

## Summer load is the real Croatian coverage test

Croatia packs a year of demand into about ten weeks, and the coast carries it. In August the population of the coastal towns multiplies, the marinas fill, and the same masts serve both. The networks are built for it, and the experience is still visibly different in mid-August than in May or October.

That matters for planning in two ways. First, a plan that works perfectly in June may feel slower in the last week of August, and that is congestion rather than coverage, so no network choice fixes it. Second, the shoulder months are the better trip for data as well as for crowds: the same plan, the same network, and far less competition for the mast.

The two independent measurements quoted on this page describe a network built for that peak. Read them as capability rather than as a promise about your week.

## Which Croatian network is best for the coast and the islands

The Croatian split is cleaner than most: Hrvatski Telekom leads on reach and on the measured speed awards, and A1 is a strong second that is often the better value in Zagreb.

### Hrvatski Telekom leads on Croatian speed and coverage

Opensignal's September 2026 report put Hrvatski Telekom top on Download Speed at 108.2 Mbps, Upload Speed at 22.0 Mbps, 5G Upload Speed and Coverage at 8.7 out of 10. It is also the network most often named as the better one on the island chains, which is the part of Croatia where a network difference actually has consequences.

### A1 Hrvatska is strong in the cities

A1 performs well in Zagreb and the coastal cities, and the report shared the Reliability award between A1 and Telemach. For a mainland itinerary it is a perfectly good foundation and, at the price most brands sell it, often the better buy.

Read the two columns as separate verdicts rather than one combined score. One comes from crowdsourced user readings and the other from the median of tests people ran voluntarily, so they can rank the same networks differently without either being wrong. Neither tells you how fast a travel eSIM will run for you, since August congestion and your handset both apply.

## Croatian SIM rules and the roaming catch at the border

Two Croatian rules shape how you buy, and the second is the one that catches people out.

| | Travel eSIM | Croatian prepaid SIM | Croatian subscription |
|---|---|---|---|
| ID required | None | Yes, passport, in a shop | Yes, with Croatian documents |
| eSIM availability | Standard | Common at carrier shops | Yes |
| Works across the EU | Only with a Europe plan | Yes, on standard cards | Yes, under fair-use limits |
| Works in Bosnia or Montenegro | Only with a Balkans or Europe plan | No, charged as roaming | No, charged as roaming |
| Best for | Almost every short trip | Longer stays wanting a Croatian number | Residents |

The registration picture is simpler than in Colombia or South Africa: a passport is enough, there is no proof of address, and the whole thing takes a few minutes at a carrier shop or a newsstand. The tourist packages sold at those counters are the cheap route into local data, and their small print is the part to read.

The catch is the border. Croatia is in the EU and the eurozone, and a standard Croatian SIM card carries EU roaming rights under fair use. The cheap tourist packages are a separate product and do not always include them, so it is worth asking before you buy one. What applies to everyone is the edge of the bloc: Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro and Serbia are outside the EU, so roaming there is charged separately whether your SIM is Croatian or your own. Watch for accidental attachment to a Bosnian or Montenegrin mast on the southern Dalmatian coast and on the Peljesac peninsula, and switch off automatic network selection if you would rather not pay for it.

## 5G in Croatia and how to plan around the water

Both Croatian networks run commercial 5G, and every Croatian carrier profile we track lists it, so there is no 4G-only option to avoid. Hrvatski Telekom's 108.2 Mbps download and 22.0 Mbps upload are mature-network numbers rather than a rollout in progress, and its Coverage Experience score of 8.7 out of 10 describes how widely the coverage actually sits.

What makes Croatia is the geography. The coast is a strip of land with a mountain range behind it and a thousand islands in front, and the network follows the strip. Coverage is excellent in the towns and along the coast road, good on the inhabited islands, and absent in the Dinaric mountains of Lika and Gorski kotar, which sit between Zagreb and the sea. Plitvice and Paklenica are covered at the entrances and thin on the trails. Download the offline map for whichever park you are walking in.

An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card it accepts, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), so it works with whatever bands your handset covers. The [phone compatibility checker](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) covers specific models.
