## How the German prepaid registration law actually works

Start with the rule, because it decides more purchases in Germany than any speed test. Identity verification has been mandatory for every prepaid activation since 2017, and the regulator that enforces it is the [Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/EN/Home/home_node.html). In practice you satisfy it one of three ways: your passport checked at a shop counter, a Video-Ident video call with a live agent, or a PostIdent appointment at a post office. None of it is difficult, and none of it needs a German address, tax number or bank account. All of it costs time you would rather spend elsewhere, and all of it happens after you land rather than before you fly.

A travel eSIM never enters that process. You buy from an international provider, scan a QR code, and connect — no document, no agent, no counter. For a five-day city break that is the whole argument, and it is why a Germany eSIM outsells local prepaid among short-stay visitors even though local SIMs are cheaper per gigabyte.

The second thing to know is that the network question is settled before you ask it. All {{< count-providers >}} brands we track ride the same three carriers — Telekom, Vodafone and O2 — as the brand table below shows row after row. No provider can sell you stronger German coverage than another, so the decision narrows to price, data shape and support. Our [comparison of Germany eSIM plans](/compare/germany/) ranks them on exactly those three.

## Telekom against Vodafone and O2 on speed and rural reach

Germany's three national networks are unusually far apart on performance for such a dense market.

### Telekom is the German 5G benchmark

Opensignal named Telekom Germany's Best Network, winning 11 of 14 awards outright, including Download Speed at 68.9 Mbps ahead of Vodafone's 56.3 and O2's 47.4. Ookla measured a Speed Score of 77.9 for the first half of 2026 against Vodafone's 56.1. Its autobahn and rural coverage is why it is the safe pick for a driving holiday.

### Vodafone is the German city performer

Vodafone runs strong 5G in urban areas and across the west of the country, sitting clearly second on both firms' tables. The countryside behind the cities is where it falls behind Telekom.

### O2 is the cheap German 5G option

O2 is the price play and sits third on speed at 47.4 Mbps in the same cycle. It has improved substantially and still trails in rural areas and indoors. Berlin, Hamburg and Munich are fine. Bavaria and the Black Forest are where it shows.

A note on how to read those two columns. They are produced by different methods, so subtracting one from the other means nothing at all. Treat each as its own ranking of the same three carriers. Neither one predicts your own throughput, which also depends on congestion, your device and the bands it supports.

## Crossing a German border with no roaming charges

Germany sits in the middle of Europe and a lot of itineraries do not respect the border. Two different things happen depending on what you bought.

A **German local SIM** includes European Union free roaming, so it keeps working across the EU and EEA subject to fair-use limits on how much of the allowance you spend abroad. A **Germany-only travel eSIM** does not. It is provisioned for German networks and stops at the border, which matters if your trip touches Salzburg, Strasbourg, Zurich or Amsterdam. In that case buy a Europe plan and confirm the country list before you pay — our network pages for [Austria](/compare/austria/), [the Netherlands](/networks/netherlands/) and [France](/networks/france/) cover the carriers on the other side.

## A Germany eSIM against a registered prepaid data plan

A German local SIM is worth the effort only for a stay of a month or more, because the identity check is the friction. Here is what the process involves.

| | Travel eSIM | German prepaid SIM |
|---|---|---|
| Identity check | None | Passport or national ID, by law |
| How you verify | Not applicable | Shop counter, Video-Ident call, or PostIdent at a post office |
| German address needed | No | No |
| When it works | On landing, any hour | After verification completes, which can mean the next day |
| Where to buy | Online, before you fly | Supermarkets, electronics chains, carrier shops, some airports |

The friction is not the paperwork itself but the sequencing. Verification can take hours to clear, shops keep German Sunday hours, and a late arrival can leave you without data until the next morning. A travel eSIM removes that dependency and costs more per gigabyte for the privilege — the trade most short-stay visitors happily make.

## What to check on your handset before a German 5G trip

All three German carriers run commercial 5G and every German carrier profile we track lists it, so there is no 4G-only trap to fall into. Opensignal measured Telekom's 5G download at 173.8 Mbps in November 2025, well ahead of Vodafone's 154.3 and O2's 114.5 — real throughput rather than a marketing label.

That makes 5G a poor reason to pay more in Germany. The checks that actually matter are narrower. Does the plan list 5G. Does your handset cover the German bands. And can it make VoLTE calls at all, because Germany has already completed its 3G shutdown and a non-VoLTE phone simply will not register on the network. An eSIM profile travels with your device rather than the SIM tray, which is convenient, but the radio support is a property of the handset — the underlying standard is documented by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/). Run your model through the [phone compatibility checker](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) before you travel.

Beyond that the German market is about where you are going rather than what you buy. City 5G is plentiful and fast on all three networks, and the rural routes are where Telekom separates itself. Live discounts sit on the [eSIM deals we track](/esim-deals/), and the [trip cost calculator](/tools/) will size an allowance to your dates.

A last pair of checks before you commit. German spectrum is licensed nationally, which is why the bands in use differ from those in North America and why an imported handset can behave differently here. Upload throughput also deserves a glance if you plan to send photos or join video calls, since German networks are tuned primarily for download.
