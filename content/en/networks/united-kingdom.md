## What Brexit changed about no roaming in Europe

Start with the rule that surprises the most visitors, because it is a genuine break with the rest of Europe.

The UK left the European Union's free-roaming arrangements when the transition period ended, and no legal obligation now requires UK operators to offer surcharge-free roaming in the EU or EEA. In practice most UK networks charge a daily fee for European use rather than including it, which means a UK SIM bought locally is the wrong purchase for a trip that continues into France, Spain or Ireland. Anyone who last travelled with a British SIM before 2021 is working from outdated expectations.

The same applies in reverse. A UK-only travel eSIM is provisioned for British networks and stops at the border, and crossing to Dublin or Paris is a separate country in mobile terms even when it is a short hop in travel terms.

That single rule reshapes the purchase. If your trip is the UK alone, buy a UK plan. If it is a European tour that starts or ends in London, buy a Europe regional plan and confirm the country list before you pay. ## Why the UK is one of the easiest places to buy data

Now the good news, which is the opposite of Germany, Spain and Thailand.

The UK has no identity registration requirement for prepaid SIM cards. No passport, no Video-Ident call, no counter queue for paperwork, no registration portal. You buy a pay-as-you-go SIM at a shop or an airport, or you install a travel eSIM before you fly, and either works immediately. Ofcom, which regulates UK communications, even runs a public [mobile coverage checker](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/coverage-and-speeds/ofcom-checker) that shows signal by provider for any postcode — useful if you are staying somewhere specific rather than moving around.

| | Travel eSIM | UK prepaid SIM |
|---|---|---|
| ID required | None | None |
| Registration | None | None |
| Works across the EU | Only with a Europe plan | Only with a roaming add-on that costs extra |
| When it works | On landing, any hour | Immediately after purchase |
| Best for | Trips up to a few weeks | Longer stays, or anyone who wants a UK number |

Because there is no friction either way, the choice comes down to price and convenience rather than paperwork. UK prepaid SIMs are competitive for longer stays, and a travel eSIM wins on the first hour after landing and on the fact that it is bought before you leave.

## Where EE leads and where O2 and Three answer

The British market has one clear leader and a clear split behind it.

### EE wins almost everything in the UK

Opensignal's January 2026 report gave EE 11 awards outright, including Download Speed at 53.2 Mbps, Reliability at 915 points out of 1000, and Consistent Quality at 78.6%. Ookla's drive testing found it won or shared every UK-wide RootScore for a 26th consecutive cycle, at a 130.3 Mbps median download — more than twice the next best result. If your trip includes the Scottish Highlands, rural Wales or Northern Ireland, EE's reach is the reason you want it under your plan.

### O2 owns UK coverage as a category

O2 won Coverage Experience outright at 9.0 out of 10, and is the only network other than EE to win anything in that cycle.

### Three owns raw UK 5G speed

Three took 5G Download Speed at 187 Mbps and 5G Upload Speed at 20.2 Mbps.

### Vodafone won nothing outright in the UK

Vodafone took no outright award in the same report, which is worth knowing before paying a premium for it. It has since merged with Three, so the market's shape is changing under the brands.

The two columns are not a single verdict. One comes from crowdsourced user readings, the other from controlled drive tests using calibrated equipment. The second is closer to what a road trip actually feels like, which is why the two can disagree on how wide EE's lead really is. Neither predicts your own speeds, because congestion and your handset both matter.

## Coverage in Scotland Wales and Northern Ireland

The UK is compact, but its coverage is not uniform, and the pattern matters for anyone travelling beyond England's cities.

| | What to expect | Which network |
|---|---|---|
| London and the major English cities | Fast 5G on all four networks | Any |
| The English countryside and the coast | Good 4G, growing 5G | EE or O2 |
| The Scottish Highlands and islands | Coverage in towns and along main routes, gaps in between | EE |
| Rural Wales and the Welsh valleys | Solid in towns, thinner between them | EE |
| Northern Ireland | Good coverage, with cross-border effects near the Republic | EE or O2 |

The consistent answer away from the cities is EE, which is why the network question in the UK has a simpler answer than in most destinations. The one thing to watch is the Irish border, where phones in border counties can drift onto a Republic of Ireland network — on a UK-only plan that means either no service or an unexpected charge.

The UK's 3G networks have largely been switched off, with Vodafone, EE and Three completing their shutdowns and O2 finishing in early 2026. All four plan to retire 2G by 2033, so the direction of travel is one-way. A 4G or 5G handset with VoLTE is unaffected. An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card it accepts, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), and it uses whatever network technology your handset supports. The [is your phone compatible](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) confirms the model.

## 5G in the UK and what to check before you buy

All four networks run commercial 5G and every UK carrier profile we track lists it, so the UK has no 4G-only option and 5G is not worth a premium. What does differ is what the headline number describes. Three's 187 Mbps is a raw 5G download figure, while EE's award count reflects consistent experience across every category and every technology.

That distinction is the one to carry into a purchase. A plan that rides Three may post the fastest 5G number while delivering a weaker experience outside the cities, and a plan that rides EE will post a lower headline speed while holding a usable connection more reliably in more places.

Practically, UK coverage is good enough that the plan matters more than the network for most trips. Two closing checks. UK spectrum is licensed by Ofcom, and the bands in use differ from those in North America, so an imported handset is worth verifying before you travel. UK SIMs also come with no fair-use guarantee for European travel, which is the practical consequence of leaving the EU roaming framework.
