## Why Spain has four networks and three owners

Four carriers run national networks in Spain, but only three companies own them. It is the only destination we track where four carriers carry travel eSIM traffic, and the four are not four independent companies.

Orange and Yoigo both sit inside the MasOrange joint venture. Their coverage footprints overlap heavily as a result, and Yoigo behaves like a budget brand riding a large network rather than a small operator with its own masts. That is genuinely useful for a visitor, because it means the cheapest plan is not automatically a coverage downgrade the way it often is elsewhere.

Movistar and Vodafone remain independent national networks. So the real choice in Spain is between three coverage footprints at four price points — and the regulator overseeing all of them is the [CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/).

On top of that, all {{< count-providers >}} travel eSIM brands we track connect to all four carriers, as the brand table below shows row by row. No provider can sell you better Spanish coverage than another, which pushes the whole decision onto price and plan shape. Our [Spain plan comparison](/compare/spain/) ranks them on exactly that basis.

## Spanish SIM registration and the one document you need

Spanish law requires prepaid SIMs to be registered to an identified person at the point of sale, and the carrier records your name, passport number and an address. Bring an original passport rather than a copy.

| | Travel eSIM | Spanish prepaid SIM |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | None | Name, passport number and address recorded |
| Document needed | None | Original passport |
| Works across the EU | Only with a Europe plan | Yes, under fair-use limits |
| When it works | On landing, any hour | After a counter visit |
| Best for | Short trips and late arrivals | Stays long enough to want a Spanish number |

Visitors from the European Union should check their own tariff before buying anything. Roam-like-at-home may already cover Spain at domestic rates, which makes a Spanish plan redundant unless you need a local number or a longer validity window. For everyone else the choice is between a travel eSIM and a counter purchase, and the eSIM wins on time even when the local SIM wins on price.

## Which Spanish network is best on speed and where the others answer

Spain's measurements are unusually lopsided at the top.

### Movistar is the Spanish benchmark

Opensignal's April 2026 report named Movistar Best Network with 10 awards outright and 3 joint, and Download Speed Experience at 96.7 Mbps — roughly 30 Mbps clear of Orange in second. Ookla separately named it Spain's Best Mobile Network and Best 5G Network for the first half of 2025, with a 102.94 Mbps median download and a 191.62 Mbps median 5G download. Its rural coverage is the widest of the four and the reason it is the safe pick for the interior and the north.

### Orange is the Spanish value second

Orange took Download Speed at 66.8 Mbps in the same cycle and is often the better value, since it sits inside a group with the scale to price aggressively. In Opensignal's April 2026 cycle a challenger brand, DIGI, took both 5G speed awards with 260.4 Mbps on 5G download — which is a useful reminder that a single headline number rarely describes a whole network.

### Vodafone keeps a smaller Spanish 5G footprint

Vodafone covers cities and the coast well but sits behind the leaders on 5G reach.

### Yoigo is the cheap Spanish entry point

Yoigo is the budget entry point into the same group as Orange, which is why it is not the coverage compromise a budget brand usually is.

Treat the two columns as independent opinions rather than a single score. They are assembled differently and can place the same four networks in a different order without either being wrong. Neither predicts your own speeds, which also depend on congestion and on your handset.

## Island and mainland coverage across Spain

Spain's geography splits into several very different coverage problems, and the network question changes with each.

| | What to expect | Which network matters |
|---|---|---|
| Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Seville | Fast 5G on all four networks | Any |
| The Mediterranean and Atlantic resort coasts | Solid 5G and 4G throughout | Any |
| Rural interior, Castile, Extremadura | Coverage thins quickly outside towns | Movistar |
| The Pyrenees and the northern green belt | Mountains and low population make it patchy | Movistar |
| The Balearics and the Canaries | Well covered on the main islands | Any, with Movistar ahead on the smaller ones |

The pattern is consistent. Cities and coasts are settled, and the further your trip moves inland or into the mountains the more Movistar's reach matters. Since every brand we track offers Movistar, that is a check on the plan rather than on the provider.

Vodafone Spain shut down its 3G network in 2024, and Movistar begins its own 3G retirement in October 2026 with completion targeted for the end of 2027. A 4G or 5G handset with VoLTE is unaffected. An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card it accepts, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), and it inherits whatever network technology your handset supports. The [check your phone](/guides/esim-compatibility-check/) covers the model-level detail.

## 5G in Spain and what to check before you buy

All four Spanish carriers we track list 5G, so there is no 4G-only network in the market and 5G is not a differentiator between brands. Movistar leads the overall 5G experience metrics, while DIGI took the raw 5G speed awards in Opensignal's April 2026 cycle. That gap between "best experience" and "fastest headline" is worth understanding before you pay a premium for a speed figure you may never reproduce.

That leaves three practical checks. Does the plan list 5G. Does your handset support the Spanish bands. And does the plan cover the countries on your route, because a large share of European itineraries cross a border and a Spain-only eSIM will not follow you into Portugal or France.

Upload throughput matters for video calls and photo uploads, and Spain's networks handle both comfortably in the cities even when download figures hog the headlines.
