## Why speed is not the problem in Georgia

Georgia is not a market where you have to shop for speed. Ookla's [Speedtest Global Index](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/georgia) put Georgia's median mobile download at 229.03 Mbps in August 2026, which is 19th of the markets it ranks and ahead of Belgium, Sweden and New Zealand.

That reframes the decision. In most destinations on this network map the first question is whether the networks are fast enough. Here they are, on all three carriers, in every city a visitor is likely to reach. The variables that actually move a Georgia trip are terrain, host network and paperwork.

One caveat is worth stating plainly. Georgia has no Opensignal Mobile Network Experience report, so there is no independent crowdsourced per-carrier ranking to set beside the other pages in this network map. The Ookla figure is a country-level median: it tells you the market is fast, and it does not tell you which carrier to prefer. For that, the carrier profiles below are the honest source.

## Where the Caucasus decides your coverage

Georgia's population sits in the valleys and along the coast, and coverage follows it. Tbilisi, Batumi and Kutaisi are covered on all three networks with 5G in the centres, and the Black Sea strip holds up along the whole coast.

The picture changes as soon as a road climbs.

| | What to expect | Which network |
|---|---|---|
| Tbilisi, Batumi, Kutaisi | Fast 5G on all three networks | Any |
| The Black Sea coast and the main east-west highway | Reliable 4G and 5G | Any |
| The Georgian Military Highway towards Kazbegi | Coverage for most of the drive, with gaps at the Jvari Pass and in the deeper valleys | Magti |
| Mestia and the Svaneti valleys | LTE in the village centres, nothing reliable above them | Magti |
| The Kakheti wine country | Good coverage on the main roads | Any |
| High alpine trails | Assume nothing and carry offline maps | Magti |

Magti operates the widest footprint of the three, which is why it stays the answer once a route leaves the cities. Because every brand we track offers it, that is a check on the plan rather than on the brand.

## Which Georgian network to ride

Georgia's three networks separate outside the cities far more than inside them.

### Magti has the widest reach in the mountains

Magti is the network for the Kazbegi road, the Svaneti valleys and the mountain routes generally. The range we track runs from about 10 to 150 Mbps with 5G in the cities, and it is the host most worth checking for if your itinerary is the reason you are buying data at all.

### Silknet is strong in the cities

Silknet is a close second in Tbilisi and the larger cities, with a similar 5G profile to Magti, and its national coverage has been improving. For a trip that stays urban it is a straightforward choice.

### Beeline is the budget option

Beeline runs 4G rather than 5G at the speeds we track, from about 8 to 80 Mbps. It is fine in the cities and thin in the mountains, which makes it a reasonable pick for a short city stay and a poor one for a driving route.

## Registration rules and why the eSIM route is simple here

Georgia is one of the easier places in the region to buy data, and the reason is what you do not have to do.

| | Travel eSIM | Georgian prepaid SIM |
|---|---|---|
| ID needed | None | Yes, passport, in person |
| Where you buy it | Online, before you fly | Carrier store or kiosk |
| Activation | Scan a profile and switch it on | Counter staff, then top up |
| Works south of the border | Only with a regional plan | No, Georgia only |
| Best for | Almost any short trip | Stays long enough to want a local number |

Georgian law ties every SIM and eSIM issued by a Georgian carrier to a passport, and one passport can register several lines. That applies to the carriers rather than to an inbound travel eSIM: a travel eSIM is issued by a provider outside Georgia and registered to that provider rather than to you, which is why there is no counter visit. ## What to plan for beyond the cities

Two things change a Georgia trip more than the plan you pick: where you drive, and whether you cross a border.

Download the regions you are visiting as offline maps before you leave Tbilisi. The passes are where a connection stops, and the failure is geographic rather than commercial, so no plan in this comparison fixes a valley. The same logic applies to sizing your allowance: mountain days use more data than city days because maps stay open, which the [trip data calculator](/tools/) handles for your dates.

Crossing into Armenia or Azerbaijan ends a Georgia-only plan. Both crossings are common on Caucasus itineraries, so price a regional or multi-country plan before you buy, and treat the country list as the thing to check rather than the brand.

Georgia's networks are still moving, too. 5G is live on Magti and Silknet in the cities, and the spectrum in use is licensed nationally across bands that some imported handsets do not tune, so an inbound eSIM can deliver less than the headline figure.
