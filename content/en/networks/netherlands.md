## Why the Netherlands has almost no coverage problem

Most destinations on this site have a coverage question. The Netherlands barely does. The country is small, densely populated and flat, the three national networks all cover it heavily, and the practical gaps that exist elsewhere in Europe — mountain valleys, long empty rural stretches, distant islands — are largely absent.

That changes the shape of the buying decision. There is no meaningful "which network reaches the places I am going" question for a trip that stays inside the country. The remaining variables are speed, price and one regulatory quirk with real consequences. The Dutch digital infrastructure inspectorate is the [RDI](https://www.rdi.nl/), which oversees the market.

All {{< count-providers >}} travel eSIM brands we track connect to the same three networks — KPN, Vodafone and Odido — which the brand table below shows row by row. No provider can sell you better Dutch coverage than another, so the brand decides the bill and nothing else. Our [comparison of Netherlands eSIM plans](/compare/netherlands/) ranks them on that basis.

## Odido is the rebrand of T-Mobile Netherlands

The Odido name deserves thirty seconds, because it confuses people reading older plan terms.

Odido is what T-Mobile Netherlands became after combining with Tele2. Both brands took the Odido name on 5 September 2023. There is no fourth network hiding behind the name — it is the same operator with three generations of branding attached to it, and travel plans sold before the change may still say T-Mobile or Tele2 on the plan description.

That matters because Odido is also the fastest of the three networks, so a plan labelled with the older name is not an older or lesser product. It is the same carrier that leads the Dutch speed tables.

The other two are straightforward. KPN is the incumbent and the coverage benchmark. Vodafone is the third national network, strongest in the Randstad cities where most visitors spend their time.

## Which Dutch network is best for speed and coverage

The Dutch split is cleaner than most, with Odido ahead on speed and KPN ahead on coverage and voice quality.

### Odido leads on Dutch 5G speed

Opensignal's March 2026 report put Odido top on Download Speed Experience at 146.4 Mbps, ahead of KPN's 101.2 and Vodafone's 92.9, and it also led the Video and Games categories. Ookla separately measured a 216.3 Mbps median download for the first half of 2025, making it the fastest provider across all technologies. It is the network to be on if speed is the priority.

### KPN leads on Dutch coverage and voice

KPN won Coverage Experience at 9.4 out of 10 and 5G Coverage Experience at 8.3, and topped Voice App Experience at 83.8 points — the measure that covers call quality on apps like WhatsApp rather than on the cellular voice network. Time on Network came down to a statistical tie, 99.3% against Odido's 99.1%. For a trip that ranges beyond Amsterdam and Rotterdam, KPN is the safer foundation.

### Vodafone is the Dutch third option

Vodafone performs well in the Randstad and sits behind the other two on the speed tables. It remains a national network with full coverage of the country, so the gap is one of degree rather than reach.

Read the two columns as separate verdicts rather than one combined score. One is built from crowdsourced user readings, the other from the median of tests people ran voluntarily, so they can rank the same three networks differently without either being wrong. Neither says how fast a travel eSIM will run for you, since congestion and your handset both apply.

## Dutch SIM rules and why prepaid eSIM is hard to find

Two Dutch rules shape how you actually buy and register data, and the second one is unusual.

| | Travel eSIM | Dutch prepaid SIM | Dutch subscription |
|---|---|---|---|
| ID required | None | Not by law, sometimes by store policy | Yes, with a BSN and a Dutch bank account |
| eSIM availability | Standard | Rare, mostly subscription-only | Yes |
| Works across the EU | Only with a Europe plan | Yes, under fair-use limits | Yes, under fair-use limits |
| Best for | Almost every short trip | Longer stays wanting a Dutch number | Residents |

The registration picture is more relaxed than in Germany, Spain or Belgium, where prepaid SIMs must be registered against a document. The Dutch state does not require it, though individual shops sometimes ask as their own policy, so carrying a passport is sensible rather than mandatory.

The eSIM picture is the opposite. Dutch carriers predominantly offer eSIM on subscriptions rather than prepaid, and a subscription needs a citizen service number and a Dutch bank account. For a visitor that effectively means the eSIM route runs through an international travel eSIM provider rather than a local network, which is the reverse of the advice that works in Germany or Thailand.

## 5G in the Netherlands and how to plan around a small country

All three Dutch networks run commercial 5G and every Dutch carrier profile we track lists it, so there is no 4G-only option and 5G is not a premium worth paying. Odido's 146.4 Mbps download and 216.3 Mbps Ookla median both reflect mature 5G rather than a rollout in progress, and KPN's 5G Coverage Experience score of 8.3 out of 10 describes how widely it is actually available.

What makes the Netherlands unusual is how little geography there is to plan around. Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague and Utrecht sit within an hour of each other and are covered identically by all three networks. Day trips to the coast and the flower fields are the only routes where the network question has any substance, and even there the differences are small enough to ignore for a short trip.

KPN and Vodafone have already switched off 3G, and KPN plans to retire 2G on 1 December 2027, so a 4G or 5G handset with VoLTE is the right device to bring. An eSIM is a profile your phone downloads rather than a card it accepts, a standard maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), and it works with whatever bands your handset covers.
