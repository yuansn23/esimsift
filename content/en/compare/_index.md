## eSIM cost per GB and cost per day explained

Travel eSIM marketing pages quote one number, usually the entry price of the smallest plan. That number is real, but it is close to useless for choosing: a $0.51 plan is a 500MB trial, not a trip.

So every row on this site carries two derived figures, computed with the same formula for every provider.

**Cost per gigabyte** is the listed price divided by the data allowance. It is the number that tells you whether a 20GB plan is better value than ten 2GB plans, and it is the column that decides the default sort order on every country page.

**Cost per day** is the listed price divided by the validity in days. It is the number that matters when you are buying unlimited data, where there is no gigabyte count to divide by.

Where a provider sells unlimited plans, we mark them and list them last rather than pretending they can be ranked by the gigabyte. Their real limits live in the fair-use policy, and we decode those in a separate table on each country page.

## Why travel eSIM prices differ by country

Three things, in order of impact.

**Wholesale rates in the destination.** A travel eSIM brand is effectively an MVNO: it buys capacity from the local carriers and resells it. Destinations where wholesale data is expensive — island nations, countries with a single dominant carrier, markets with heavy roaming regulation — start higher and stay higher, no matter which brand you pick.

**How many brands compete there.** Popular destinations carry eight competing brands and sharp prices. Thin markets carry two or three, and the spread between them widens. The card grid above is sorted so you can see which countries have the deepest lineup.

**Whether the plan is metered or unlimited.** Unlimited-by-day plans are priced for heavy users and are usually the wrong buy for a light trip: at a typical day rate, two weeks of unlimited costs more than a metered bucket that would never have run out. The crossover point is on every country page.

## Local SIM vs roaming vs eSIM

A travel eSIM is not automatically the cheapest option — it is the cheapest option that works before you land. Where a country sells prepaid SIMs freely at the airport, a local SIM can undercut a travel eSIM on price; the trade-off is the queue, the registration rules and the phone number you lose. Where roaming from your home operator is included in your plan, roaming can beat both on convenience. Our destination pages tell you which case you are in, and the country quirks section on each page covers ID rules, registration and coverage traps.
